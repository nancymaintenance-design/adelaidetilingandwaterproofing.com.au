Wall tile substrate preparation and feature tile alignment

Wall tiling can create a practical finish or a feature surface. We assess the wall lining, existing coating and the proposed tile size and weight before confirming preparation. Tiles should not simply cover a loose, damaged or unsuitable wall base.

The layout considers the wall dimensions, visible cuts, corners and terminations. Large-format tiles, small formats and patterns place different demands on the base and set-out, so send the selected tile details if you have them. Specific material suitability is confirmed for the job rather than assumed.

For wet bathroom walls, use the bathroom tiling scope. Around benchtops and outlets, our kitchen splashback tiling page covers that separate room-specific preparation.

Wall substrate, tile weight and edge finishes

  • Wall lining, paint or existing tiles and required preparation.
  • Selected tile dimensions, weight and layout.
  • Cut positions, exposed edges and junction finishes.
  • Whether the wall is internal, external or exposed to water.

Electrical, plumbing or structural changes are separate trade scopes, confirmed before installation.

Wall tile substrate preparation

Old paint, adhesive residues and damaged wall lining can affect the suitability of the base. We assess what needs removal or preparation and whether the wall can support the proposed tile size and weight. A new tiled surface should not conceal unresolved dampness or a loose coating. The quote identifies the accessible wall area and any lining work included.

Feature tile alignment and visible edges

The selected format changes the balance of cuts at corners, openings and terminations. Share tile dimensions and the intended pattern so we can agree alignment, edge trims and visible cuts before installation. Fittings and outlets need a coordinated sequence with the relevant trades. For wet walls, membrane preparation is confirmed separately from the decorative or practical tile finish.

Wall finish and tile selection

Tell us which wall will be tiled, its existing finish and any selected tile size or format. We check the base, tile weight and edge details.

Fittings, corners and adjoining surfaces guide the cuts and layout; product details can follow the first enquiry.

Ceramic, porcelain and mosaic feature wall finishes

We install ceramic wall tiles, porcelain feature tiles and mosaic details behind vanities, in shower niches and across feature walls. We plan visible cuts, corner returns and trim profiles before fixing. The tile dimensions and weight guide the wall preparation and installation method, while the layout keeps pattern changes and feature strips aligned.

Glass mosaic tiles need a product-compatible adhesive and grout, with particular care around mesh backing, visible adhesive marks and sheet joints. A marble-look porcelain tile is porcelain, not natural marble. Share the product and the wall location so we can plan the right preparation, cuts and finished edges for your chosen finish.

Wall glaze, water spots and stone-look finishes

We compare ceramic wall tiles, porcelain wall tiles and glass mosaics by the actual body, surface and backing. Smooth glazed finishes can simplify wiping; strong relief and extra mosaic joints add detail to the cleaning routine. Dark, glossy and textured samples may show water deposits differently, so compare a sample where it will be used.

Marble-look porcelain is not natural marble, and mosaic describes a format rather than one material. We check tile weight, wall condition, adhesive suitability and exposed edges before agreeing the installation. For natural stone, share the exact stone type and care instructions so fixing and maintenance can be reviewed before the scope is confirmed.
