Bathroom strip-out, waterproofing and retiling

We inspect the exposed base, repair the substrate within the agreed scope and coordinate drainage, membrane installation and the new tile finish. We confirm fixture positions, tile thickness, floor levels and thresholds before construction.

You can contact Ellis before choosing or buying tiles. We confirm your selection and its effect on levels and drainage before the screed and waterproofing details are finalised.

Before removal, identify what stays: the shower location, vanity, doorway and adjoining floor level. Keeping a layout still needs a check of the waste position, floor falls and the space available for the new finish. A change to tile thickness or a larger tile format can affect the build-up, cuts and drainage detail.

Once the base is exposed, we check its condition before covering it again. Loose bedding, damaged lining or an unsuitable base can change the preparation needed. The written scope records the known work and how any concealed damage will be assessed, priced and approved.

For an existing shower leak or limited repair, see bathroom waterproofing and shower leak repairs.

Preparation, waterproofing and installation

  1. Confirm the layout. We agree drainage, tiles, floor levels and the shower threshold.
  2. Prepare the base. We inspect the open substrate, repair and prepare the agreed area. Additional work requires written approval before it proceeds.
  3. Install and check. We install the selected system, schedule curing and complete the required inspection or testing.
  4. Finish and hand over. We complete the bathroom tile installation, coordinate later fittings and provide care and return-to-use instructions.

For apartments and units, we confirm access, any building approvals, shared drainage arrangements and permitted work hours before installation.

Confirm who supplies and fits the waste, plumbing fixtures, electrical fittings and shower screen before setting the work dates. The waterproofing and tiling sequence depends on those positions being ready; later drilling, moving fittings or changing levels can affect completed details. We agree the handover points for related trades in the quote.

For an occupied home, plan how the room will be isolated, where materials and removed finishes can pass through, and which bathroom will be available during the work. In a unit, confirm the building contact and access arrangements early so common-area protection and deliveries can be organised.

Plan bathroom downtime and cost factors

The room can be unavailable through removal, base repairs, membrane application, curing, tiling and final fitting. We set the proposed sequence after assessment and confirm when each stage is ready for the next. Product requirements, site conditions and trade availability affect the dates; there is no single duration that suits every renovation.

Quote differences often come from the amount of removal, substrate repairs, drainage changes, tile supply, format and cuts, access and disposal. Compare the described work and exclusions as well as the total. If the open base reveals extra work, we explain the revised scope, cost and timing for your approval before proceeding.

Check the handover before using the room

We review the agreed finish, junctions and any outstanding items, and provide the care and return-to-use instructions for the installed materials. Confirm what installation records are included and keep the product information with the quote.

Later fittings need care around completed surfaces. Share the relevant installation details with anyone fitting accessories or screens, and raise any uncertain fixing position before drilling.
