Bathroom renovation waterproofing, from assessment to completion

Ellis Services Group provides professional waterproofing for bathrooms that are in use, stripped back, under renovation or ready for tiled finishes. We assess shower areas, baths, vanities, wet locations and existing surfaces, then complete the waterproofing or repair work required.

What makes a renovation different

In a repair, the question is how little can be opened up. In a renovation, the question is the opposite: everything is already open, so the decisions made now determine whether the finished room lasts. The membrane is a small part of the budget and a large part of the risk, because it is the one part of the job that is invisible once the room is finished.

The practical consequence is that a renovation has more ways to go wrong than a repair. A shower position moved after the screed, a tile chosen after the membrane, a floor level that ends up above the door threshold — each of those is a sequencing problem rather than a waterproofing problem, and each one costs more to fix than to prevent.

The decision order in a bathroom renovation

Renovation decisions have dependencies. This planning order identifies decisions to settle; confirm the membrane and screed construction sequence for the selected system and applicable compliance pathway:

  1. Confirm layout, fixture positions, drainage and finished levels.
  2. Agree plumbing rough-in and inspect the exposed substrate.
  3. Confirm preparation, falls and the selected membrane and screed assembly.
  4. Complete that assembly in its specified sequence, including junctions, corners and penetrations.
  5. Allow product curing and complete agreed inspection or testing.
  6. Release the work for tiling and grouting, with protection of the waterproofing.
  7. Complete flexible joints, fixtures and screens, then confirm readiness for use.

The layout and drainage decisions come first. The construction sequence then follows the design, selected system and applicable project requirements. Tiling starts after agreed readiness checks and handover.

Lock the tile choice before waterproofing

Tile selection looks like a finishing decision. It is not. Tile thickness and format affect the depth of the tile bed, which affects the finished floor level, which in turn interacts with the shower hob or threshold, the door clearance and any step at the entry. A change of tile after the membrane is down can mean the detail at the threshold no longer works.

You do not need to have paid for tiles before you talk to us, but the size and thickness should be decided rather than left open. If the tile choice is still moving, say so — it is better to know before the screed than after.

The renovation sequence and other trades

A bathroom renovation is a chain of trades, and the waterproofing sits in the middle of it. Each handover is a point where work can be scheduled in the wrong order.

Who does what, and when

Strip-out exposes the substrate so preparation and related plumbing work can be confirmed. The specified assembly determines the order of screed and membrane work. The critical handover is release for tiled finishes after product curing and any required inspection or test. If you engage separate trades, agree that release point before booking the tiler.

Where both scopes sit with one provider, that coordination is handled for you. You can see the finished-surface side of the work on our bathroom tiling services page and the wider wet-area side on our waterproofing services page.

Four handover points to confirm

1. Layout, drainage and tile thickness

Before construction details are fixed, confirm fixture and waste positions, tile thickness and format, finished floor levels and the entry threshold. The relevant plumbing and tiling decisions need to support the waterproofing detail. If a selection changes, have its effect on levels and drainage reviewed before the next stage proceeds.

2. Inspection of the open substrate

After removal, Ellis checks the accessible surfaces and identifies preparation needed for the agreed work. Damp, damaged, loose or uneven substrate can change the scope. Record the finding, explain repair or replacement needs and their cost and timing, then obtain written approval for additional work before covering the area. Keep the update with the original quotation.

3. Curing and the selected inspection or test

Agree the selected system, its curing requirements and any inspection or test required by the project. Product instructions and site conditions affect readiness. As a product-specific example, the ARDEX flood-testing technical bulletin discusses cure readiness for its membranes. Your project's schedule follows the selected manufacturer's requirements and site conditions.

Where flood testing is specified, confirm suitability, timing, responsibility and records with the team. Other projects may have a different agreed verification arrangement. Do not fill a suspect wet area yourself to test it.

4. Release to the tiler

Confirm that preparation, membrane details, curing and agreed checks are complete before tiled finishes cover the work. Record the release and any protection requirements. Discuss how screens, fittings and later trade work will avoid damaging or penetrating completed waterproofing without an agreed detail.

The ABCB waterproofing FAQ explains the different membrane-position pathways and the need to check state or territory requirements for existing-building upgrades. It discusses Housing Provisions, not a single rule for all Adelaide renovations. Confirm the applicable South Australian project requirements and selected system during planning.

Local surface repair or full renovation?

Replacing an identified surface seal is a smaller scope than removing tiles, preparing the substrate and renewing waterproofing with new finishes. Ellis Services Group checks the seals, concealed membrane access, drainage falls and any related plumbing work on site, then confirms the repair path and quote. Ask the quotation to name the affected area and the intended outcome so different scopes can be compared fairly.

Strata and body corporate approvals

If the bathroom is in an apartment or unit, there is usually a layer of process before the trades start. Renovation work in a strata property commonly needs approval, and the building may set rules on access, lift use, parking, waste removal and when noisy work can happen. Shared drainage and the position of the floor waste are building-level questions rather than unit-level ones.

Worth checking before demolition rather than during it. If the approval process has a timeline of its own, it is easier to plan around it at the start.

What a renovation waterproofing scope covers

A written waterproofing scope for a renovation should separate the stages, so you can see what is included and compare quotes on the same basis:

  • Strip-out of the existing tiles, bed and fittings.
  • Substrate inspection, and repair or replacement where it has been affected.
  • Screed to falls around the waste.
  • Primer to the prepared substrate.
  • Junctions, corners, penetrations and drainage connections detailed for the selected system.
  • Membrane installation to the specified coverage and assembly.
  • Cure time as specified by the membrane manufacturer.
  • Inspection or testing agreed for the selected system and project requirements.
  • Documentation of what was done and what the test showed.

If a quote bundles all of that into one line, it is hard to tell which preparation, screed work or testing is included. Ask for each stage and any exclusions to be stated.

Common renovation waterproofing questions

Does waterproofing go down before or after the screed?

The membrane may be above or below the tile bed or screed where the applicable construction and compliance pathway permits. Confirm the design and selected system before work starts. The ABCB Housing Provisions FAQ allows either position under its stated provision, while an AS 3740 pathway must be checked against that standard. There is no single membrane position that applies to every renovation.

Can I keep the existing shower position?

Often yes, if the existing waste and falls still work for the new layout. If the waste has to move, that is a plumbing change and it happens before the screed and membrane, not after.

Do I have to move out during the work?

Confirm bathroom downtime from removal through curing, agreed testing, tiling and finishing. The selected products and site conditions determine when the room is ready for use. Whether you can remain in the property depends on the facilities available and the agreed programme.

What if the existing waterproofing looks fine?

Ellis Services Group inspects the wet area, confirms which layers will be removed and explains the waterproofing renewal in the written renovation scope. If removal exposes extra substrate preparation, we obtain written approval before that work proceeds.

What happens if damage is found after strip-out?

Ellis explains the exposed condition and the additional preparation or related trade work needed, then confirms the cost and timing effect. Additional scope requires written approval before it proceeds. Keep that approval with the original quote and handover records.
