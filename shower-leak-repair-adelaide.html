Shower Leak Repair Adelaide

A leaking shower needs a repair that addresses the cause. Ellis assesses the accessible shower and adjoining areas, then explains whether a local joint repair or removal and waterproofing renewal suits the findings.

What a shower leak repair can include

Water beside the shower, damp skirting or a stain on the ceiling below can come from different sources. Worn seals, damaged grout, loose tiles, drainage problems and concealed waterproofing damage need different responses. We define the repair area after assessment rather than assuming every shower needs a full rebuild.

  • Inspection of accessible tiles, grout, flexible joints and shower edges.
  • Local regrouting or resealing where the condition supports that repair.
  • Tile removal and base preparation where access beneath the finish is needed.
  • Membrane renewal and coordinated tile reinstatement within the agreed scope.

Plumbing or other work outside the proposed repair is identified before the scope is agreed. Tell us if a plumber has already investigated the leak.

Assessing where the water is coming from

Tell us when the dampness appears: during shower use, after use or even when the shower has been unused. We look at accessible surfaces and the adjoining damage, and discuss the shower's repair history. A stain shows where water has appeared; it does not, by itself, prove which layer has failed.

Concealed conditions cannot always be established from the tiled surface. If opening the affected area or further investigation is needed, we explain what that involves before proceeding. Photos of visible damage can help the initial discussion, but the on-site findings determine the repair recommendation.

Choosing the repair and reinstatement sequence

For worn surface joints on a sound base, shower regrouting and resealing may be the appropriate scope. New grout or silicone does not renew a failed membrane beneath the tiles. If the concealed waterproofing needs attention, the work may require tile removal, substrate repairs and a membrane system compatible with the prepared base.

We agree the removal and reinstatement boundaries before work. Preparation, waterproofing, curing and the agreed readiness checks come before the tiled finish. Where the damage or intended changes extend across the room, see bathroom waterproofing or renovation waterproofing.

What affects the quote and shower downtime

The size of the affected area is only one factor. Access beneath the finish, the condition of the base, the extent of moisture damage and matching replacement tiles can change the work required. The quote identifies removal, preparation, materials and finishing so you can see what the repair covers.

A local joint repair and a membrane renewal follow different schedules. Drying conditions, material curing and any agreed checks affect when the shower can be used again. We confirm the expected downtime for your scope; if removal exposes additional damage, changes to work, price and timing are discussed before that extra work proceeds.

Returning the shower to use and watching the repaired area

Keep the shower out of use for the period confirmed for the installed materials. At handover, we explain care for the grout, seals and tiles, including cleaning guidance relevant to the finish. Do not judge readiness only by how dry the surface looks.

Let us know if dampness returns, spreads or appears elsewhere. A surface repair should not be repeatedly used to cover an unresolved concealed leak. For general questions about leak repairs and curing, read the waterproofing and leak repair FAQs and quotes and handover FAQs.
