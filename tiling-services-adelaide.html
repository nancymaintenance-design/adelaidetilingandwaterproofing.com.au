Bathroom and shower tiling: layout, niches and wet-area preparation

Bathroom tiling brings the wall and floor finishes together around the shower, vanity and fittings. We plan the tile layout, cuts, corners and visible edges before installation, including niches or other details where they form part of the agreed work.

For an ensuite update or a shower retiling project, the existing tiles and base need checking before the replacement finish is agreed. New tiles alone do not renew concealed waterproofing. We coordinate any required bathroom waterproofing with the tile installation; an active leak follows our shower leak assessment and repair route first.

See the recorded North Terrace shower renovation stages for a verified Ellis team project, rather than assuming every bathroom needs the same scope.

Bathroom tile layout and wet-area junctions

  • Wall lining, floor base and any removal needed.
  • Tile format, set-out, corners, edges and fittings.
  • Shower floor falls, outlets and adjoining floor heights.
  • Membrane stages, grout and flexible junctions included in the quote.

Tell us whether the bathroom is still in use and whether you have noticed a leak, loose tiles or damage.

Balcony retiling, doorway transitions and exterior tile suitability

Balcony tiles need to work with the exposed base, drainage and doorway transition. We assess the balcony before planning tile removal or new installation, with attention to outlets, edges, movement junctions and the finished height at the door.

If water ponds or reaches the room below, another layer of tiles is not a diagnosis or a waterproofing repair. The existing build-up may need investigation and removal before the tiled finish can be agreed. Our balcony waterproofing service explains the connected membrane and drainage assessment.

For a strata property, share the authorised contact and access arrangements before booking. We describe the proposed work without deciding ownership boundaries or who must pay.

Balcony thresholds, outlets and exterior tiles

  • Base condition and whether old tiles need removal.
  • Outlet access, drainage direction and door thresholds.
  • Exterior tile suitability, edges and flexible joints.
  • Waterproofing work, preparation and use restrictions within the agreed scope.

Tell us whether the balcony is covered, how it is accessed and when any water issue occurs.

Courtyard and alfresco tiling with drainage and movement joints

We tile courtyard and alfresco floor areas, planning the finish around outdoor use, adjoining paths and doorways. The assessment looks at the existing base, levels and where surface water can drain before tile layout and preparation are confirmed.

Outdoor tile selection is more than choosing a colour. We check the proposed material for the location and use, alongside the available product information, cleaning needs and edge details. A textured appearance alone does not establish slip performance.

Movement joints and connections to the building need a deliberate detail rather than being covered with ordinary grout. Any required waterproofing is assessed for the particular construction; not every outdoor area uses the same system.

Alfresco drainage, edges and movement joints

  • Courtyard base, cracks and existing finishes.
  • Doorway transitions, drainage and adjoining paths.
  • Exterior tile selection, layout and exposed edges.
  • Preparation, movement joints and access during the work.

Include steps or changes in level in your enquiry so their suitability and details can be assessed separately.

Wall tile substrate preparation and feature tile alignment

Wall tiling can create a practical finish or a feature surface. We assess the wall lining, existing coating and the proposed tile size and weight before confirming preparation. Tiles should not simply cover a loose, damaged or unsuitable wall base.

The layout considers the wall dimensions, visible cuts, corners and terminations. Large-format tiles, small formats and patterns place different demands on the base and set-out, so send the selected tile details if you have them. Specific material suitability is confirmed for the job rather than assumed.

For wet bathroom walls, use the bathroom tiling scope. Around benchtops and outlets, our kitchen splashback tiling page covers that separate room-specific preparation.

Wall substrate, tile weight and edge finishes

  • Wall lining, paint or existing tiles and required preparation.
  • Selected tile dimensions, weight and layout.
  • Cut positions, exposed edges and junction finishes.
  • Whether the wall is internal, external or exposed to water.

Electrical, plumbing or structural changes are separate trade scopes, confirmed before installation.

Pool surround tiling, coping edges and drainage

We provide pool surround tiling for the walking surfaces and terraces around a pool. The layout and preparation account for coping edges, adjoining paving, drainage, movement junctions and the connection to the existing pool edge.

Poolside materials need to suit their location and use. We consider the selected tile and its product information with the expected wet conditions, cleaning and exposed edges before agreeing the finish.

This service covers pool surrounds, not tiling or waterproofing inside the pool. Pool-interior or water-retaining work needs a separate suitability assessment and confirmed scope. A pool surround photograph is not evidence of a pool-interior membrane installation.

Pool coping and surrounding level connections

  • Existing terrace base, tile removal and adjoining levels.
  • Coping, exposed edges and the pool-edge connection.
  • Surface drainage, movement joints and selected material suitability.
  • Access and protection of the pool during the agreed work.

Tell us whether the enquiry concerns the surround, coping or pool interior so the work is assessed correctly.

Other wall and floor tiling areas

For a laundry, entry or another tiled area, tell us the surface, its condition and how the space is used. We assess whether the job is a new tiled finish, retiling or a smaller repair, then set out the preparation and installation required.

Internal floor work, including connected-room layout and floor height transitions, is explained on our floor tiling page. A laundry with wet-area requirements also needs the relevant laundry waterproofing scope considered before finishes are installed.

Worn joints do not always mean the whole surface needs retiling. For shower joints, compare shower regrouting and silicone resealing; for loose or cracked tiles, start with local tile repair. Recurring moisture needs its source assessed, not covered with a new finish.

Room use, surface condition and preparation

  • Room or outdoor location and wall or floor area.
  • Loose tiles, damaged joints, moisture or an unfinished base.
  • Existing and proposed finishes at doors and fittings.
  • Access, material responsibility and any separate trade work.

Photos are useful but optional. You can enquire without measurements or a final tile selection.

Tile regrouting for walls and floors

Missing or deteriorated grout can leave a tiled surface difficult to maintain. Ellis assesses the joints and surrounding tiles before agreeing regrouting for the affected wall or floor area. Loose tiles, movement or moisture damage may need separate repairs rather than simply new grout over the visible problem.

We remove the included deteriorated grout, prepare the joints and install the agreed replacement material. The selected grout needs to suit the tile, joint and use of the space; we confirm the material and finish for the project rather than promise one product for every surface.

Grout between tiles and flexible silicone at movement junctions have different roles. For a shower, see our shower regrouting and silicone resealing service. Neither renews a failed concealed membrane; recurring leaks need their source assessed first.

Grout condition, joint preparation and material choice

  • The affected wall or floor area and condition of its tiles.
  • Grout joints to renew, removal and preparation.
  • Replacement grout suitability, colour and any flexible junctions.
  • Access, protection and product-specific return-to-use instructions.

For cracked or loose tiles, compare tile repair and replacement before choosing joint-only work.

How we plan your tiling work

Assess the base and layout

We check the existing finish, base, dimensions, access and adjoining surfaces. Removing old tiles, repairing the substrate or changing levels is identified before the new finish is agreed; hidden findings may require a revised scope.

Confirm the written scope

The quote identifies preparation, tile supply or client-supplied tiles, installation, edges and joints. Confirm removal, material responsibility, waste disposal and separate trades rather than assuming every quote includes them. Read our tiling quote comparison guide.

Install and explain care

We prepare the agreed base, set out and install tiles, then complete the specified grout and flexible junctions. Return-to-use timing follows the installed materials and site conditions, not one universal number of hours. See our drying and curing guide.
