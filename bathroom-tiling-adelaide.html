Bathroom and shower tiling: layout, niches and wet-area preparation

Bathroom tiling brings the wall and floor finishes together around the shower, vanity and fittings. We plan the tile layout, cuts, corners and visible edges before installation, including niches or other details where they form part of the agreed work.

For an ensuite update or a shower retiling project, the existing tiles and base need checking before the replacement finish is agreed. New tiles alone do not renew concealed waterproofing. We coordinate any required bathroom waterproofing with the tile installation; an active leak follows our shower leak assessment and repair route first.

Bathroom tiles, niches and membrane handover

The tile set-out starts with visible wall lines and the floor layout together, rather than treating each surface separately. We check niche positions, trim thickness and cuts around fittings against the selected tile format. A niche needs its prepared and protected base agreed before tiles are fixed; the tile finish does not establish that concealed junctions are watertight.

Retiling and access to the existing base

Removing old tiles can expose damaged lining or disturb the membrane below. The written scope identifies the removal boundary and how unexpected findings will be handled before the new finish proceeds. Tell us which fittings remain, whether the shower is leaking and whether other rooms need to stay accessible. Plumbing changes and fitting relocation are agreed as separate trade work.

Porcelain floors, ceramic walls and mosaic shower niches

We lay porcelain floor tiles and ceramic or porcelain wall tiles in bathrooms and ensuites. We set out the room around the shower, vanity and niche so tile lines continue cleanly across adjoining surfaces. Large-format tiles need the base and cuts planned early; mosaic sheets need careful spacing at sheet edges and inside corners.

For shower tiling, we plan the tile format around falls to the floor waste, edge cuts and the waterproofing handover. Ceramic and glass mosaics have different backing and adhesive requirements. Bring your chosen tile or product details when discussing the room, and we can include preparation, set-out, grout and flexible junctions in the written quote.

Wet bathroom floors and easy-clean wall tiles

We select the bathroom floor finish using the tile product’s wet-area slip information, the shower layout and the planned falls to the waste. Matt or textured porcelain may be worth comparing, but appearance alone does not confirm grip. Tile size, cuts at the waste, grout joints and cleaning access all affect the installation.

For shower walls, we compare smooth glazed ceramic and porcelain with the intended cleaning routine. A surface that wipes clean easily can still show dried water or soap deposits. We plan the wall joints, mosaic niches and flexible corner seals together; the tiled finish does not replace the waterproofing behind it.
