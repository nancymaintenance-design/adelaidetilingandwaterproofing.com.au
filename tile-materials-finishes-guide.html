Choosing Tiles for Bathrooms, Kitchens and Pool Surrounds

Choose the tile body, finish and installation location together. We compare wet-floor suitability for bathrooms and pool surrounds, easy-clean wall surfaces and kitchen splashback care, then plan the base, adhesive, joints and edges for the selected product.

Separate material, surface, format and intended use

Porcelain and ceramic identify tile bodies; polished, matt, gloss, satin and lappato describe finishes. Rectified describes an edge treatment, while subway, mosaic and large-format describe shape or format. Pool and outdoor describe uses that still need product suitability checked. A full-body porcelain label describes the body construction, not a guarantee of grip or stain resistance.

Porcelain is a type of ceramic material, although Australian retail ranges commonly list porcelain and ceramic separately. For the tile often called 玻化砖 in Chinese, confirm the supplier’s porcelain designation; vitrified describes vitrification and is not an interchangeable name for every porcelain product. Polished describes surface polishing, glazed describes a glaze layer, and full-body or through-body porcelain describes colour or composition through the body. A glazed porcelain tile can also be polished; a glossy glaze does not itself mean polished. Glass mosaic is glass, not vitrified porcelain. Send the actual product specification, size and intended location so we can plan the installation.

Bathroom floors and pool edges need wet-use evidence

Start with the product’s slip-test information for the actual wet walking area and the intended use. A matt or structured porcelain finish can be a candidate, but the finish name alone does not establish suitability. At a pool, also compare barefoot comfort, sun exposure, the coping profile and cleaning. Different slip-test methods should not be treated as interchangeable labels.

We plan the tile format and cuts around floor wastes, falls, thresholds and movement joints. A wall tile, waterline mosaic or pool-interior tile is not automatically suitable for the surrounding walking surface. The installation and maintenance need to support the selected surface.

Wall cleaning and kitchen splashes shape the surface choice

Smooth glazed ceramic and porcelain are useful comparisons for shower walls and kitchen splashbacks. Deep relief and extra mosaic joints can take more attention to clean. Glossy surfaces may show dried droplets; an easy-wipe glaze does not prevent every water mark, soap deposit or cooking stain. Review a sample and the supplier’s care information.

We check cement-based or epoxy grout against tile compatibility, joint width and the selected product’s stain and cleaning information. Flexible seals at corners and benchtops serve a different purpose from grout. Materials beside a cooktop also need to suit the appliance instructions and project requirements; tile appearance alone does not settle that detail.

Natural stone and look-alike tiles have different care

Marble-look or travertine-look porcelain is not marble or travertine. Real terrazzo is not terrazzo-look porcelain. Bluestone is a stone trade name: many Australian products are basalt, but the supplier’s actual stone identification still matters. A confirmed porcelain slab or panel is manufactured porcelain, not natural stone; large-format describes size rather than a new material. Product names need to be read alongside the specification.

Marble, limestone and travertine can react to acidic cleaners. Some stone, cement-based terrazzo, cement and terracotta products specify sealing and particular care. A sealer can be part of surface care, but it is not a wet-area membrane or a guarantee against every stain. Confirm the exact product before choosing cleaners or adding a treatment.

Bring the product specification into the written scope

Share the supplier’s product name, actual material, size, thickness, finish, intended room and any slip or care information. Tell us if you already own the tiles. We assess the base, material compatibility, layout, grout and flexible joints, edge pieces and any specified sealing before confirming the installation scope.

If you are considering natural stone or another finish beyond the named porcelain and ceramic products, send its specification so we can review the preparation and fixing before agreeing the work. You can enquire before selecting or buying tiles.
